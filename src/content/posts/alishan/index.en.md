# Background History of the Alishan Mianyue Line

The Alishan Mianyue Line is a railway built during the Japanese colonial era to haul the giant trees that were being logged. It later developed into a tourist attraction, with small trains carrying passengers. The Mianyue Line is one of the branch lines, ending at Shihou (Stone Monkey) Station. It's "9.2 kilometers" long in total and crosses "24 bridges and 12 tunnels" along the way.

![](./7.jpg)

In 1999 the 921 Earthquake caused landslides on the line and it stopped running. After some repairs it briefly reopened, but in 2008 it was damaged again by Typhoon Morakot (the 88 Flood) and has never resumed service since. Thanks to its flat route and beautiful scenery, it has become a famous hiking spot.

Suggested items to bring:
1. Headlamp: there are lots of tunnels and they're really dark, so bring one
2. Hiking shoes or rain boots: the trail is muddy in many places. I sincerely suggest you don't wear New Balance like I did; washing them afterwards was painful
3. Trail snacks: chocolate, bread, salt candy and so on, to refuel anytime
4. Water: lots of people online recommend 2,000 cc, but it depends on the person. 600 cc was enough for me
(These suggestions are for a day hike; if you plan to camp, check other sources)

Because of a string of recent accidents, the Alishan Forestry Railway and Cultural Heritage Office decided to close the trail for renovation from January 17 to April 30, 2022, replacing and widening the existing wooden boards to make them less slippery.
No wonder the hike went so smoothly for us. It's super safe compared to before

# Summary

I'm a total newbie hiker, so this rating is purely personal. I'd say the difficulty is low to medium. The Mianyue Line is 9.2 km long with only about 100 meters of elevation change, so it's gentle and easy to walk. In summer the temperature up in the mountains is around 10-something degrees Celsius, which is super comfortable. We set off around 11:00 and reached the end a little after 2:00, so one way takes about 3–4 hours. On the way back we heard thunder, so we picked up the pace and made it back to "Zhaoping Station" in half the time.
