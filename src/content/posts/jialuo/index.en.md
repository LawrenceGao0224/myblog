# About Jialuo Lake

Jialuo Lake sits in Siji Village (四季村), Datong Township, Yilan County, in the Jialuo Mountain area along the northern branch of the Central Cross-Island Highway. It's located where Nanhu Mountain (南湖大山) and Sanxing Mountain (三星山) of the Central Mountain Range meet, on the ridge running from north of Liluo Mountain (里洛山) to Taipingshan. It's named after nearby Jialuo Mountain, runs north to south, and is the source of Jianafu Creek (加納富溪). At 2,242 meters above sea level, Jialuo Lake is actually part of a whole group of lakes — besides Jialuo Lake itself there's Retreat Pond (撤退池), Haomai Pond (豪邁池), Weidan Pond (偉蛋池), Ink Pond (墨池), Sun Lake, Moon Lake, Heavenly Pond (天池), Cypress Pond (檜木池), Water Pond (水池), Green Pond (綠池) and more, with 18 alpine lakes found so far. Jialuo Lake is the biggest of them all, about 300 meters long, 150 meters wide and 15 meters deep.
The Legend of Jialuo Lake – "The Scattered Pearls"
The legend of Jialuo Lake is pretty magical. Long, long ago there was a beautiful fairy. One day, while she was doing her makeup, she got distracted and accidentally smashed the mirror on her table. The glittering, diamond-like shards rained down over the Jialuo Mountains and became this huge cluster of dreamlike lakes. With their crystal-clear water, lots of sunlight and beautiful surroundings, the Jialuo lakes became known as "The Scattered Pearls".
Source: travelking.com.tw

# How Jialuo Lake Was Rediscovered in Modern Hiking History

In January 1987, five members of the National Taiwan University Mountaineering Club formed the first expedition to scout and summit Jialuo Lake and Jialuo Mountain. On the southwest ridge of Jialuo Mountain, near what's now Retreat Pond, they took the wrong trail and couldn't make it up the mountain. They camped, turned back the next day, set off again, and only then found the right path and made it into the Jialuo Lake area. That night they camped beside the first basin pond they came across and named it "Jialuo Lake". But when they kept going the next day and ran into a much wider lake, the team changed their minds and gave the name "Jialuo Lake" to that big lake in front of them (today's Jialuo Lake). As for the little pond where they'd camped after getting lost and nearly calling it quits the day before, they gave it a name to remember all the ups and downs of the trip: "Retreat Pond".

# Itinerary

![2](./2.jpeg)

I'd recommend doing it over two days. A same-day round trip is really tough, especially since it rains a lot in the Yilan mountains. When you arrive in the afternoon, the fog might be hiding the view of Jialuo Lake, and it may not clear until the next morning.
Day 1: Siji Forest Road → Iron Gate → Jianafu Creek → Giant Tree Trailhead → Retreat Pond → Weidan Pond → Jialuo Lake
Day 2: Jialuo Lake → Weidan Pond → Retreat Pond → Giant Tree Trailhead → Jianafu Creek → Iron Gate → Siji Forest Road

# Applying for a Mountain Entry Permit

Apply 5 to 30 days before your trip on the [National Police Agency website](https://nv2.npa.gov.tw/NM107-604Client/), or on the day of your hike, get your permit at any of these Yilan County Police Bureau offices: Sanxing Precinct, Datong Police Station, Taipingshan Police Station, Mingchi Police Station, Su'ao Precinct, Aohua Police Station or Bihou Police Station. [(Source: Yilan County Police Bureau)](https://www.ilcpb.gov.tw/?aspxerrorpath=/main/page_view.aspx) 

# What to Bring

1. Trail snacks (high-calorie chocolate, energy bars, bread...)
2. At least 2000cc of water recommended
3. Salt candy (for electrolytes)
4. Warm clothes (it gets really cold up there — when I went, it dropped to -2°C around midnight. I also recommend wearing two layers of socks)
5. Trekking poles
6. Rain boots (tall ones recommended, a lot of the trail is super muddy)
7. Tent, sleeping pad
8. Toiletries
9. First-aid supplies
(PS: If you're a beginner, I'd recommend booking with a porter service for meals and renting a tent and sleeping pad to lighten your backpack)
(PS: Dress in layers — you'll get really hot on the climb, so you want clothes that are easy to take on and off)

# Summary

I'm a total hiking newbie, so this is just my personal rating, but I'd say the difficulty is medium-high. It's 9K one way, 18K round trip, so make sure you train before you come — don't underestimate it!
